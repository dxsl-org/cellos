C2C Tier-2 fixture resolved by name, and a stale descriptor refused (x86_64 QEMU, 2026-10-09)
=============================================================================================

What this records
-----------------
Two things, on the same boot:

1. The Phase-02 cross-tier exchange is now **registry-named**. The Tier-2 provider is
   registered under the fixture's service id by **its spawner** (`init`), and the Tier-1
   driver resolves it through the shipped SDK — no tid handed over, no argv handoff.
2. Slice A's stale-descriptor rule gets its **first runtime witness**: once the provider
   is gone, the descriptor the driver's handle holds is refused rather than re-targeted.

Why the earlier reading ("a private-root service needs an authority decision") was wrong
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
`RegisterService` is `SpawnCap`-gated, so a private-root Cell cannot register **itself** —
which is what the fixture's first version did not do, and the driver therefore addressed a
tid `init` passed it. But the *spawner* holds `SpawnCap`, and registering on behalf of a
cell it spawned is a pattern this tree already uses: `spawn_hypervisor()` registers the
hypervisor service for the tid it just spawned (`cells/tools/init/src/boot.rs`). The kernel
takes the liveness check and the identity capture in one scheduler lock hold, so the entry
carries the provider's real `(cell_id, generation)` — no caller-supplied identity, no new
authority, no ABI, no kernel change.

Reading it
----------
  * **Named, with the provider's own identity.** `PROVIDER-BINDING tid=9 cell=5 gen=142` is
    a private-root Cell's binding as the registry states it: `cell=5` is that Cell and
    `gen=142` its epoch, captured by the kernel — not a value the provider or the driver
    asserted. `PROVIDER-NAME-MATCHES-RAW=true` is the SDK's `ServiceRef` resolution
    agreeing exactly with a raw `LookupServiceBound` read, so both paths see one binding.
  * **The exchange is unchanged in substance** — typed 512-byte request answered with a
    verified length and checksum, the Tier-2 cell calling the *named* Tier-1 VFS service
    from inside its private root, the oversize frame refused before delivery, the
    unauthorized method refused with no side effect.
  * **The stale descriptor is refused.** After the provider serves its budget and exits,
    the driver polls the registry through the raw opcode (deliberately not through the SDK,
    whose cache this leg is about) until no live binding remains (`PROVIDER-GONE=none`).
    The next call then goes through the cached descriptor: the send is refused, the SDK
    classifies it against the registry and reports `NotFound` — `STALE-BINDING=REFUSED` —
    and drops the descriptor (`STALE-BINDING-CLEARED=true`). Nothing is delivered to the
    retired tid and nothing is silently re-targeted onto another incarnation; had either
    happened, the leg prints `STALE-BINDING=SERVED` and the lane fails on it.

What this does not claim
------------------------
  * Still outstanding from the phase's four pre-delivery refusals: a wrong **user buffer**
    on the syscall copy path (needs a raw-pointer fixture; `/bin/tier2-exploit` already
    witnesses the address-containment class on this lane).
  * No ABI, authority, kernel or loader change: the fixture's service id is a literal in
    init's feature-gated block and in the fixture's own crate, cross-referenced; a drift
    fails the driver's first resolution loudly instead of silently.
  * The bounded/async lifecycle is untouched — this exchange is still the synchronous
    copied typed call, and Phase-03 step 3 owns moving it.
